Most owners only look at their IT when something has already gone wrong. For Northern Kentucky businesses, reliable IT support can help prevent those problems before they become expensive disruptions. A file won't open, a laptop won't start, or an invoice gets paid into a scammer's account. Fixing it at that point costs more than preventing it would have.

Most IT problems don't happen without warning. The backup that fails when you finally need it may have been failing for weeks. The account a scammer used may belong to someone who left last year. For a Northern Kentucky small business, spending just 30 minutes a month checking your technology can help catch those problems early.

## Why a monthly look is worth the time

Verizon's [2026 Data Breach Investigations Report](https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/reports/dbir/) found that 31% of breaches started with attackers exploiting software that hadn't been patched. That makes unpatched software the most common way in, ahead of stolen passwords. The same report found the median time to fully fix a known problem has risen to 43 days.

Most attacks use a problem that was already known, with a fix already available. Nobody had installed it yet.



## The 30-Minute IT Check

![Northern Kentucky small business IT support and cybersecurity check on a laptop](https://res.cloudinary.com/dygso04l2/image/upload/v1790334233/agence-olloweb-d9ILr-dbEdg-unsplash_ccdcz6.jpg "Northern Kentucky Small Business IT Check")

### 1.Check for Updates

Check whether Windows updates are installing on your computers, or sitting at "restart required" week after week. Do the same for phones and for the software you use most, like your browser and your accounting app. If people keep clicking "remind me later," that's something to fix.

### 2. Verify Your Backups

Open your backup tool and look at the last few runs. You want recent successful backups, not a list of errors. Then check when anyone last restored a file from it. If it's never been tested, you don't know whether it works.

### 3. Review Who Has Access

Pull up the list of user accounts in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace and read through it. Every name should be someone who still works for you. Look for people who left, contractors who finished months ago, and shared logins like "office" or "admin" that several people use. Switch off anything you don't need.

### 4. Check Multi-Factor Authentication

Check that MFA is switched on, and that it's on for everyone, not just the people who set it up first. Pay closest attention to admin accounts and anyone who handles money. [Microsoft's research shows MFA blocks more than 99.2% of account compromise attacks.](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/authentication/concept-mandatory-multifactor-authentication)

If someone can't enable MFA or keeps running into authentication problems, that's a good reason to involve your IT provider.

### 5. Review Connected Devices

Look at what's connected to your systems. If there's a laptop or phone you don't recognize, find out whose it is. While you're there, check that laptops are encrypted and that any phone with company email on it has a passcode or fingerprint lock.

### 6. Review Subscriptions and Licenses

Open your billing page and read what you're paying for. Businesses regularly pay for licenses belonging to people who left, or for two tools that do the same job. It's also how you find software somebody signed up for without telling anyone.



## Make Your IT Check a Monthly Routine

Put it in the calendar on a fixed day, like the first Monday of the month, and give it to the same person each time. That's you or whoever handles the admin side.

Keep a running note of what you checked and what you found. After a few months you'll see whether the same problem keeps coming back. If it does, it needs fixing properly instead of clearing each time.

Thirty minutes only works if you don't stop to fix things along the way. Write down what you find and deal with it afterward.

## Know When to Call Your IT Provider

Most findings will be small. A laptop needs restarting, a license needs canceling, or an old account needs to be switched off.

Handle those yourself if you're comfortable doing so.

Other problems deserve help from an IT support provider in Northern Kentucky, including:

* Backups that keep failing
* MFA that won't turn on for a user
* A device nobody recognizes
* Updates that repeatedly fail on the same computer
* Accounts with unusual or excessive access
* Security issues you aren't sure how to resolve

Those problems can indicate something bigger happening behind the scenes.

## What a Monthly IT Check Doesn't Do

It isn't monitoring. A good IT provider has tools watching your systems all day and flagging things you'd never spot from a monthly glance.

The check covers what those tools can't know. You know who left, which subscriptions you approved, and whose laptop is whose.
