Ask five Cincinnati MSPs what IT support costs and you'll get five versions of the same answer: *"It depends — let's schedule a call."*

It does depend. But "it depends" is also how you end up three sales calls deep with no number. So here's the straight version, including our own pricing — which most providers in this market won't put in writing.

## The Three Ways Cincinnati Businesses Pay for IT

### 1. Managed IT services (fixed monthly fee)

This is the model most businesses with 5+ employees land on: a flat monthly fee that covers monitoring, maintenance, help desk, security, and support. You're paying for problems to be *prevented*, not just fixed.

**Typical Cincinnati market range: $100–$250 per user, per month**, depending on what's included and who you ask.

**Our pricing: $99–$199 per user per month.** In practice that works out to:

| Business size | Typical monthly investment |
|---|---|
| 5–15 employees | $1,500 – $2,500 |
| 15–30 employees | $2,500 – $4,500 |
| 30+ employees | Custom quote |

What's included matters more than the number. At those rates, our clients get 24/7 monitoring, a 15-minute response guarantee (in writing — more on that below), cybersecurity protection, Microsoft 365 management, help desk, and backup/disaster recovery. When you compare providers, compare the *included list*, not just the per-user rate — a cheap seat price with security sold separately usually isn't cheap.

### 2. Break-fix (hourly)

You call when something breaks; you pay by the hour. Cincinnati-area rates typically run **$100–$250/hour** depending on the shop and the urgency.

Break-fix looks cheaper until the month something real happens. A server failure that takes two days to resolve isn't just 16 billable hours — it's two days of your team not working. And structurally, break-fix providers earn more when you have *more* problems. That incentive never sits right with us, and it's the main reason we only work on flat monthly agreements.

Break-fix can still make sense if you're under ~5 employees with genuinely simple needs. Above that, the math almost never holds.

### 3. Projects (fixed quote)

Office moves, server migrations, new phone systems, security camera installs — these are quoted per project. Any provider worth hiring will give you a fixed number in writing before work starts. If you're getting "time and materials" on a project with no cap, keep shopping.

## What Actually Moves the Price

Two 20-person Cincinnati businesses can have very different IT bills. The honest drivers:

- **Compliance requirements.** HIPAA for medical and dental practices, cyber-insurance security mandates, SOC 2 prep — regulated IT costs more because there's more to do and document.
- **Servers and infrastructure.** A business running everything in Microsoft 365 is cheaper to support than one with on-premise servers.
- **Security posture.** Basic antivirus and real layered security (MFA, email filtering, awareness training, tested backups) are different products. In 2026, we'd argue only one of them is actually optional insurance.
- **After-hours needs.** If your business runs beyond 9–5, coverage should too.

## The Questions That Matter More Than the Price

After 15+ years in this market, here's what we'd ask any provider — including us — before signing:

1. **"What's your response time guarantee, and what happens when you miss it?"** Get it in writing. Ours is 15 minutes, and if we miss it, we credit that month's service fee for the affected user or device. If a provider's guarantee has no consequence attached, it's a goal, not a guarantee.
2. **"How long is the contract?"** Most Cincinnati MSPs require 1–3 year agreements. Everything we do is month-to-month — our 98% client retention is why we can afford to work that way. A provider that needs a 3-year lock-in is telling you something about how confident they are you'd stay voluntarily.
3. **"What's *not* included?"** Onsite visits, projects, after-hours calls, security tooling — this is where "affordable" plans get expensive.
4. **"What does offboarding look like?"** The best test of an MSP is how they behave when you leave: documentation handed over, credentials released, no hostage-taking. Ask up front.
